14  Video Understanding Benchmarks

This is a companion chapter to Chapter 12. The benchmark survey lives here so that the main chapter can move quickly through architectures while still pointing to a definitive index of every evaluation set worth knowing about. The structure mirrors the task taxonomy of Section 12.2: one subsection per family of tasks, each with a few headline benchmarks expanded into fact blocks and the rest listed compactly.

Every benchmark below is written as a compact fact block with the same six fields so the section can be scanned at speed:

  • Domain — what the videos are of (third-person YouTube, egocentric cooking, movies, surveillance, …).
  • Size & length — number of videos, total hours, typical per-clip duration.
  • Task — which downstream task from Section 12.2 it scores.
  • Modalities — video only / video + subtitles / video + audio / video + audio + dense annotations.
  • Probes — what aspect of video understanding it uniquely tests; the reason this benchmark exists alongside its neighbours.
  • Importance — critical (every paper reports), standard (most papers in this sub-area report), niche (specialised but useful), saturated (SOTA too close to the ceiling to differentiate models), deprecated (still cited but superseded). Plus a note on the metric the field reports.
Note

Numbers in this section are taken from the original benchmark papers and verified against the leaderboards as of mid-2026 where possible. When a count or duration is given as approximate (∼) it is the figure reported by the paper rather than a recount.

14.1 Action Recognition Benchmarks

The oldest video task and still the default reported number for every encoder paper. The action-recognition benchmarks split into three flavours: large-scale clip classification (Kinetics family), motion- and interaction-heavy classification (SSv2, Diving48), and multi-label / spatio-temporal (Charades, AVA).

  • Kinetics-400 / -600 / -700 (Kay et al. 2017; Carreira et al. 2018, 2019) (2017–2019)
    • Domain: YouTube clips, third-person, single dominant human action.
    • Size & length: K400 ≈ 306K clips × 10 s ≈ 850 h, 400 classes; K600 ≈ 480K × 10 s, 600 classes; K700 ≈ 650K × 10 s, 700 classes.
    • Task: action recognition (clip classification).
    • Modalities: video (audio usually stripped).
    • Probes: generic action recognition at scale; the de facto pretraining benchmark for video encoders.
    • Importance: saturated on K400 (best encoders > 90 % top-1; VideoMAEv2 (L. Wang et al. 2023), InternVideo2 (Y. Wang et al. 2024)) but still the default reported number in every encoder paper. K700 is the harder, less-saturated current target. Metric: top-1 / top-5 accuracy.
  • Something-Something v2 (SSv2) (Goyal et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: crowdsourced clips of someone manipulating an object on a tabletop, filmed against a plain background.
    • Size & length: ∼220K clips × 4 s, 174 fine-grained action classes (e.g. “pushing X from right to left”, “pretending to put X on Y”).
    • Task: action recognition, motion-heavy.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: temporal understanding — the same scene can yield different labels depending solely on the direction of motion, so a model that looks at one frame cannot win.
    • Importance: critical for any paper that wants to argue its model has temporal understanding (separates frame-bag classifiers from real video models). Metric: top-1 accuracy.
  • UCF-101 (Soomro et al. 2012) (2012)
    • Domain: sports + everyday action clips from YouTube.
    • Size & length: 13K clips × 7 s, 101 action classes.
    • Task: action recognition.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: historic ImageNet-of-video; 3-split protocol still used for tiny-data ablations and zero-shot transfer.
    • Importance: saturated (top-1 > 98 %); also widely used as an FVD reference for video generation. Metric: top-1 accuracy.
  • HMDB-51 (Kuehne et al. 2011) (2011)
    • Domain: movie clips + YouTube; 51 action classes.
    • Size & length: 7K clips × ∼4 s; 3 official train/test splits.
    • Task: action recognition.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: small-scale transfer; harder than UCF-101 due to cluttered scenes and inter-class similarity.
    • Importance: saturated; legacy benchmark; usually quoted as a triplet with UCF-101 and K400. Metric: top-1 accuracy across the three splits.
  • Moments in Time (MiT) (Monfort et al. 2019) (2019)
    • Domain: very short web clips spanning humans, animals, objects, weather, abstractions.
    • Size & length: ∼1M clips × 3 s, 339 classes; a multi-label extension (Multi-Moments) has ∼1M clips with 313 classes.
    • Task: action recognition (extremely short clips).
    • Modalities: video + audio.
    • Probes: generalisation across agents (not just humans) and very short events; large training scale.
    • Importance: standard as an auxiliary pretraining corpus, less common as a final eval. Metric: top-1 / top-5 accuracy.
  • Charades (Sigurdsson et al. 2016) (2016)
    • Domain: crowdsourced Hollywood-in-Homes — people perform scripted indoor activities.
    • Size & length: ∼10K untrimmed videos × ∼30 s, 157 daily-activity classes.
    • Task: multi-label action classification and temporal localization (multiple actions can overlap).
    • Modalities: video + audio.
    • Probes: dense multi-label classification under heavy overlap; activity recognition in unscripted-looking settings.
    • Importance: standard for multi-label classification and as the source of the Charades-STA grounding split. Metric: mAP (multi-label), mAP@IoU (localization).
  • AVA (Gu et al. 2018) (2018)
    • Domain: 15-min movie segments densely annotated.
    • Size & length: ∼430 movies, ∼1.6M actor-action labels at 1 Hz, 80 atomic action classes (e.g. stand, sit, hand-wave).
    • Task: spatio-temporal action detection — per-actor bounding box + action labels.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: combines detection (where) with action recognition (what) on long-form video.
    • Importance: critical and non-saturated for any model that claims spatial-temporal localisation; SlowFast (Feichtenhofer et al. 2019) and MViTv2 (Li et al. 2022) benchmark here. Metric: frame-level mAP @ spatial IoU 0.5.
  • Diving48 (Li et al. 2018) (2018)
    • Domain: competitive diving videos.
    • Size & length: ∼18K clips × ∼5 s, 48 fine-grained dive classes.
    • Task: action recognition, motion-only (background and equipment are uninformative).
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: isolation of motion cues from scene/object bias — designed as a counter-example to Kinetics-style “context cheating”.
    • Importance: niche but the cleanest test that a model uses motion; pairs well with SSv2. Metric: top-1 accuracy.

14.2 Long-Video Understanding Benchmarks

The 2023–2026 wave of benchmarks built specifically for video VLMs with minute- to hour-long inputs. All are MCQ-style by construction (so they can be auto-scored at the LLM-judge-free level of reliability) and all emphasise aggregation and narrative reasoning over per-frame perception.

  • LongVideoBench (LVB) (Wu et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: 3 763 web videos across multiple genres, paired with their original subtitles.
    • Size & length: 8 s – 1 h per video; 6 678 human-written MCQ questions over 17 fine-grained categories.
    • Task: long-video MCQ VQA with referring-query structure — every question explicitly cites a sub-window the answer is grounded in.
    • Modalities: video + subtitles.
    • Probes: memory and segment retrieval under interleaved video+text input; the referring clause forces the model to pick from many candidate windows.
    • Importance: critical — together with Video-MME the de facto pair every long-video VLM reports (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-Video). Metric: MCQ accuracy, with separate splits by duration (8–15 s / 15–60 s / 3–10 min / 15–60 min).
  • Video-MME (Fu et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: 900 hand-picked YouTube videos spanning 6 high-level genres (knowledge, film/TV, sports, life, multilingual, artistic).
    • Size & length: ∼254 h total, durations from 11 s to ∼1 h split into short / medium / long sub-splits; 2 700 expert-written MCQs (3 per video × 4-way).
    • Task: general video MCQ VQA — the eval is also commonly reported in with-subtitles mode.
    • Modalities: video + subtitles + audio (some questions are answerable only from subtitles).
    • Probes: general video understanding across the full duration spectrum in one suite; the long sub-split (30 min – 1 h) is the de facto long-video number.
    • Importance: the most-reported video VLM benchmark of 2024–2026. Gemini 1.5 / 2.x, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5/3-VL, InternVL2.5/3, LLaVA-OneVision, Apollo, LongVU all report on it. Metric: MCQ accuracy, separately no-subs and w/ subs.
  • MLVU (Multi-task Long Video Understanding) (Zhou et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: very diverse — movies, surveillance, cartoons, games, egocentric, life vlogs, etc.
    • Size & length: ∼1 700 videos, 3 min – 2 h long; ∼3K questions across 9 sub-tasks (plot QA, action order, needle-in-haystack, sub-scene captioning, summarisation, …).
    • Task: MCQ VQA + open-ended captioning/summarisation — the only major long-video benchmark with non-MCQ sub-tasks.
    • Modalities: video only (no subtitles).
    • Probes: multi-task long-video understanding — exposes models that are tuned only for MCQ.
    • Importance: standard in 2025–2026 long-video papers; complementary to Video-MME because it has open-ended generation sub-tasks. Metric: MCQ acc for MCQ tasks; generation metrics (CIDEr / LLM-judge) for the open ones.
  • LVBench (W. Wang et al. 2024) (2024) — distinct from LongVideoBench above
    • Domain: publicly sourced long videos (TV episodes, sports, surveillance, …).
    • Size & length: ∼500 videos, mean ∼68 min, max several hours; ∼1.5K MCQs.
    • Task: long-video MCQ VQA, with a focus on hour-scale reasoning.
    • Modalities: video + subtitles.
    • Probes: the very long tail (> 30 min) where Video-MME has only ∼100 videos.
    • Importance: important for hour-scale specialists (LongVU, Video-XL-2, LongVA, LongVILA all report). Metric: MCQ accuracy.
  • EgoSchema (Mangalam et al. 2023) (2023)
    • Domain: egocentric (head-mounted camera) clips drawn from Ego4D.
    • Size & length: ∼5K MCQs over 3-minute clips, ∼250 h total.
    • Task: long-form egocentric MCQ VQA, 5-way.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: intrinsic temporal certificate — by design every question requires reasoning over the full 3 min (the certificate length is ∼10× prior egocentric benchmarks).
    • Importance: critical for any long-video or egocentric model (Gemini, Qwen2.5-VL, Apollo all report). Metric: 5-way MCQ accuracy; the subset split has 500 fully validated questions, the fullset has all 5K.
  • HourVideo (Chandrasegaran et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: 1-hour egocentric videos from Ego4D.
    • Size & length: 500 videos, mean ∼45 min, range 20–120 min; ∼13K MCQs.
    • Task: very-long-form MCQ VQA with task categories summarisation / perception / visual reasoning / navigation.
    • Modalities: video only (audio available).
    • Probes: hour-scale reasoning specifically, with a curated task taxonomy.
    • Importance: emerging — released late 2024, gaining traction in 2025–2026 long-video work. Metric: MCQ accuracy.
  • MomentSeeker (Hu et al. 2025) (2025)
    • Domain: long videos (mean 20 min) for video corpus moment retrieval (VCMR).
    • Size & length: 268 long videos, 1 800 multi-modal queries.
    • Task: locate a relevant temporal segment within a corpus of long videos (not a single video).
    • Modalities: video + audio + subtitles; multi-modal queries.
    • Probes: the corpus version of moment retrieval at long-video scale; needs efficient retrieval and fine grounding.
    • Importance: emerging — the de facto VCMR-at-scale benchmark for 2025–2026 retrieval research. Metric: R@1/R@5 at IoU.

14.3 General Video VQA and Reasoning Benchmarks

The workhorses for short / medium-clip evaluation. MVBench and the Video-MME short split are now the must-haves; the older ActivityNet-QA / MSRVTT-QA / MSVD-QA generation benchmarks are still reported but generally regarded as saturated and replaced by MCQ ones above.

  • MVBench (Li et al. 2024) (2024, introduced with VideoChat2)
    • Domain: clips reused from Charades, EPIC-Kitchens, SSv2, NTU RGB+D, Perception Test, FunQA, etc.
    • Size & length: ∼4K MCQ examples spanning 20 temporal-reasoning task types (action order, object existence, action prediction, scene transition, counterfactual, …); short clips (mostly < 30 s).
    • Task: MCQ VQA, temporal-reasoning-specific.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: designed so the answer cannot be inferred from a single frame nor from world knowledge alone — the 20 task categories are constructed to force inter-frame reasoning.
    • Importance: the canonical short-clip video VLM benchmark of 2024–2026. Every video VLM paper reports it. Metric: 4-way MCQ accuracy, with optional per-task breakdown.
  • TVBench (Cores et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: filtered subset of MVBench’s source clips with newly written questions.
    • Size & length: ∼2.5K MCQs over 10 temporal-skill categories.
    • Task: MCQ VQA, temporal-only.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: addresses a known weakness of MVBench — its questions are partly solvable from a single frame or from text alone. TVBench filters those out: most published 2024 video LMs score near chance at release.
    • Importance: standard, used alongside MVBench as a “true temporal” sanity check. Metric: MCQ accuracy.
  • TempCompass (Liu et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: ∼500 carefully edited videos (paired clips that differ only in their temporal dimension — direction, speed, order).
    • Size & length: ∼7K MCQs spanning action, direction, speed, event order, attribute change.
    • Task: MCQ VQA, temporal-only.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: controlled-pair tests for temporal sensitivity, with paired-video accuracy as an additional metric (model must answer both clips in a contrastive pair correctly to score 1).
    • Importance: standard for any paper making a temporal-reasoning claim (Qwen2.5-VL, Apollo, VideoLLaMA3 report). Metric: MCQ accuracy + paired accuracy.
  • Vinoground (J. Zhang et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: 1 000 short-clip pairs that differ only in their temporal counterfactual content (e.g. cause vs effect).
    • Size & length: ∼1K pairs × ∼5 s each.
    • Task: binary MCQ + dense temporal-counterfactual judgement.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: the temporal analogue of the Winoground image benchmark — counterfactual reasoning that requires distinguishing two near-identical clips.
    • Importance: emerging; the strongest published 2024 video LMs score below random on the paired metric. Metric: pair accuracy + group accuracy.
  • Perception Test (Patraucean et al. 2023) (2023)
    • Domain: scripted real-world “perception probes” filmed in lab settings — people manipulating objects, performing physical demos, illusions.
    • Size & length: 11 600 videos, ∼23 s each; densely annotated with 190K object tracks, 73.5K action segments, 137K sound segments, 38K MCQ-VQA + 6K grounded VQA.
    • Task: combined — VQA + temporal action localisation + object tracking + sound localisation + grounded VQA.
    • Modalities: video + audio + dense annotations.
    • Probes: a capability matrix — 4 reasoning skills (memory, abstraction, physics, semantics) × 4 reasoning types (descriptive, explanatory, predictive, counterfactual). Designed as a diagnostic, not a single number.
    • Importance: important multi-task diagnostic; widely reported in 2024–2026 video VLM papers (Qwen2.5-VL, Apollo, VideoLLaMA3). Metric: top-1 MCQ accuracy by default; per-skill breakdown.
  • MMBench-Video (Fang et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: ∼600 YouTube videos × 10 s – 6 min.
    • Size & length: ∼2K open-ended QAs across 26 fine-grained capability dimensions (counting, reasoning, OCR, captioning, …).
    • Task: open-ended VQA scored by LLM-as-judge (GPT-4 by default).
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: capability-axis breakdown for VLMs, generation-style (vs MCQ).
    • Importance: standard for VLM papers that want a generation-style number alongside MCQ. Metric: LLM-judge score (0–3) averaged per dimension.
  • NeXT-QA (Xiao et al. 2021) (2021) and NeXT-GQA (Xiao et al. 2024) (2024)
    • Domain: ∼5.4K trimmed YouTube clips of daily-life activity.
    • Size & length: ∼44s avg; ∼52K MCQs in NeXT-QA covering causal, temporal, descriptive; NeXT-GQA adds temporal grounding annotations for 5.6K causal/temporal QAs.
    • Task: MCQ VQA (NeXT-QA) and grounded MCQ VQA (NeXT-GQA).
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: causal and temporal reasoning specifically (NeXT-QA); whether MCQ accuracy is grounded in the actual evidence window (NeXT-GQA).
    • Importance: NeXT-QA standard; NeXT-GQA important and unique — it measures the grounding gap, the fraction of correct MCQs that are also temporally well-grounded. Metric: MCQ accuracy + IoU@0.5 grounding accuracy for NeXT-GQA.
  • STAR (Wu et al. 2021) (2021)
    • Domain: filtered Charades clips.
    • Size & length: ∼22K MCQs across interaction / sequence / prediction / feasibility situations.
    • Task: situated reasoning MCQ VQA, with object-relation graphs in the supervision.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: compositional and counterfactual reasoning, especially the feasibility and prediction questions which require physical-world common sense.
    • Importance: standard — frequently reported by VLM papers as an OOD reasoning test. Metric: MCQ accuracy per situation type.
  • AGQA / AGQA-Decomp (Grunde-McLaughlin et al. 2021) (2021)
    • Domain: Charades clips with auto-generated compositional questions.
    • Size & length: ∼9.6M compositional MCQs (filtered subsets of 192K and 3M widely used).
    • Task: compositional, multi-step VQA.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: compositional reasoning chains over object relations.
    • Importance: niche — auto-generated questions are sometimes noisy; cited mainly for compositional ablations. Metric: MCQ accuracy.
  • TVQA / TVQA+ (Lei et al. 2018, 2020a) (2018 / 2020)
    • Domain: clips from 6 popular TV shows (sitcom + medical + crime drama).
    • Size & length: ∼22K clips × 60–90 s; 152K MCQs; TVQA+ adds bounding boxes + frame indices for 29K of those.
    • Task: MCQ VQA (TVQA), grounded MCQ VQA (TVQA+).
    • Modalities: video + subtitles (subtitle text is often essential to answer).
    • Probes: dialogue-heavy reasoning where subtitles dominate.
    • Importance: standard for subtitle-aware VLMs; metric: MCQ accuracy.
  • ActivityNet-QA (Yu et al. 2019) (2019)
    • Domain: ActivityNet web videos.
    • Size & length: 5.8K videos × ∼2 min, 58K open-ended QAs.
    • Task: open-ended VQA (short answers, often single words).
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: generic action-and-object questions over short web videos; the historical open-ended VQA baseline.
    • Importance: standard but declining — increasingly replaced by MCQ benchmarks. Metric: accuracy (exact match by GPT-judge), sometimes WUPS.
  • MSRVTT-QA / MSVD-QA (Xu et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: MSR-VTT and MSVD web clips.
    • Size & length: MSRVTT-QA — 10K clips, 244K QAs; MSVD-QA — 1 970 clips, 50K QAs.
    • Task: open-ended VQA, short clips.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: very short web-video VQA; historical baseline.
    • Importance: deprecated for SOTA papers (saturated, single-frame solvable for most questions) but still reported as a sanity number. Metric: accuracy (GPT-judge or exact match).
  • VideoChatGPT-bench (VCG-Bench) (Maaz et al. 2024) (2023)
    • Domain: subset of ActivityNet videos.
    • Size & length: ∼3K open-ended QAs.
    • Task: generation-style VQA scored on 5 axes — correctness of information, detail orientation, contextual understanding, temporal understanding, consistency.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: dialogue quality of an end-to-end video chatbot.
    • Importance: standard in video-chat papers (VideoChat2, Video-LLaVA, LLaVA-Video). Metric: GPT-judge 1–5 scores per axis.

14.4 Temporal Grounding and Moment Retrieval Benchmarks

Given a natural-language query, find when the described event occurs. The metric of choice is Recall@N at IoU \(\tau\) (see Equation 12.2).

  • Charades-STA (Gao et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: indoor daily-activity videos from Charades.
    • Size & length: 9 848 videos × ∼30 s, ∼16K query–moment pairs.
    • Task: sentence-conditioned moment retrieval (Video Moment Retrieval / Temporal Moment Localization).
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: mid-length indoor moment retrieval; the canonical introductory grounding benchmark.
    • Importance: standard — every temporal grounding paper reports it, though now considered too short and too easy to differentiate modern systems. Metric: R@1 and R@5 at IoU ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}; mIoU.
  • ActivityNet Captions (Krishna et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: ActivityNet web videos.
    • Size & length: 20K videos × ∼2 min, ∼100K densely annotated event captions (∼3.6 captions per video).
    • Task: dense video captioning and (separately) sentence-conditioned moment retrieval.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: longer multi-event narratives; dense captioning evaluates event detection + captioning jointly.
    • Importance: critical for dense captioning, standard for grounding. Metric: dense — CIDEr / METEOR @ tIoU + SODA_c (Fujita et al. 2020); grounding — R@N IoU.
  • DiDeMo (Hendricks et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: Flickr web clips, descriptive of everyday scenes.
    • Size & length: ∼10K untrimmed videos × ∼30 s, ∼41K query–moment pairs; moments are quantised to 5-s bins (a defining feature).
    • Task: sentence-conditioned moment retrieval.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: short-clip retrieval with discretised time — simpler metric than continuous IoU.
    • Importance: standard; metric: Recall@N over bins, plus mIoU.
  • QVHighlights (Lei et al. 2021) (2021)
    • Domain: ∼10K YouTube vlogs (lifestyle, news, travel) × ∼150 s.
    • Size & length: ∼10K queries with both moment annotations (1+ relevant segments per query) and saliency scores per 2-s clip.
    • Task: joint moment retrieval and highlight detection.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: the only widely used benchmark that scores moment retrieval and highlight ranking in the same dataset.
    • Importance: critical for temporal grounding papers since 2022 (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, TimeChat). Metric: R@1 IoU={0.5, 0.7} + mAP for moments; mAP @ HIT@1 for highlights.
  • TACoS (Regneri et al. 2013) (2013)
    • Domain: cooking videos.
    • Size & length: 127 videos × ∼5 min, ∼17K query–moment pairs.
    • Task: moment retrieval, long-clip cooking domain.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: dense fine-grained step-level grounding within long cooking videos.
    • Importance: standard; metric: R@N IoU.
  • HiREST (Zala et al. 2023) (2023)
    • Domain: instructional YouTube videos.
    • Size & length: ∼3.4K videos × 0.5–8 min, multi-step retrieval annotations.
    • Task: hierarchical retrieval — moment retrieval and step segmentation and clip-level retrieval, evaluated jointly.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: hierarchical-task generalisation; multi-task evaluation.
    • Importance: emerging — used by long-video / instructional-video specialists. Metric: R@N for retrieval; step-level F1.

14.5 Video Corpus Moment Retrieval (VCMR)

Strictly a generalisation of moment retrieval: the query is matched against all moments in all videos in a corpus rather than against moments in a single given video. The metric is the same (R@N IoU) but the corpus size makes retrieval indexing essential.

  • TVR (Lei et al. 2020b) (2020) — built from the TVQA TV-show corpus; ∼22K videos × 60–90 s, ∼109K query–moment annotations across visual + subtitle + audio modalities. Modalities: video + subtitles + audio. Probes: multi-modal moment retrieval; the standard VCMR benchmark since 2020.

  • MAD (Moment Anything Dataset) (Soldan et al. 2022) (2022) — ∼650 long movies × ∼110 min from the LSMDC source, ∼384K natural-language queries from movie audio descriptions. Probes: moment retrieval over a corpus of full-length movies, where most clips are far from any given query. Importance: the canonical long-video VCMR benchmark.

  • TVR-Ranking (Lei et al. 2020b) — a re-scored split of TVR that converts the original retrieval task into a ranking problem with relevance judgments, used for evaluating rerankers.

  • MomentSeeker (Hu et al. 2025) (2025) — see Section 14.2; the modern VCMR-at-scale benchmark.

14.6 Captioning and Retrieval Benchmarks

The classical text↔︎video alignment benchmarks. Almost all of them sit between training corpus and test set — the same MSR-VTT collection appears as MSRVTT-QA above, as a retrieval test here, and as a generation test for video generators.

  • MSR-VTT (Xu et al. 2016) (2016)
    • Domain: YouTube clips from 20 categories (music, sports, news, …).
    • Size & length: 10K clips × 10–30 s, 20 captions per clip (200K captions).
    • Task: clip captioning + text↔︎video retrieval (canonical 1K-A / 1K-B test splits).
    • Modalities: video + audio.
    • Probes: general short-clip caption / retrieval; long-running leaderboard.
    • Importance: critical historical benchmark, still reported by every contrastive paper (CLIP4Clip (Luo et al. 2021), ViCLIP (Y. Wang et al. 2023), LanguageBind-Video (Zhu et al. 2023)). Metric: captioning — BLEU-4/METEOR/CIDEr; retrieval — R@1/5/10, median rank, mean rank.
  • VATEX (Wang et al. 2019) (2019)
    • Domain: Kinetics-600 clips re-captioned by humans.
    • Size & length: ∼41K clips × 10 s, ∼826K captions (10 EN + 10 ZH each).
    • Task: clip captioning + retrieval; explicitly multilingual.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: parallel EN/ZH evaluation; the standard multilingual video benchmark.
    • Importance: standard for multilingual evaluation; metric: same as MSR-VTT.
  • YouCook2 (Zhou et al. 2018) (2018)
    • Domain: instructional cooking videos from YouTube.
    • Size & length: 2K videos × ∼5.3 min, ∼14K step segments with captions.
    • Task: dense video captioning + step localisation + retrieval.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: instructional procedural understanding; pre-training signal for cooking-oriented models.
    • Importance: standard for instructional-video work (TimeChat (Ren et al. 2024), HiREST (Zala et al. 2023)). Metric: CIDEr / METEOR @ tIoU.
  • LSMDC (Rohrbach et al. 2017) (2017)
    • Domain: movie clips with audio-description tracks (subtitles for the blind).
    • Size & length: ∼118K clips × ∼5 s from 200 movies, 1 caption each.
    • Task: clip captioning, retrieval, and fill-in-the-blank.
    • Modalities: video + audio.
    • Probes: narrative-style descriptions; movie-domain bias.
    • Importance: standard but declining; metric: same as MSR-VTT.
  • MSVD (Chen and Dolan 2011) (2011)
    • Domain: very short YouTube clips of single events.
    • Size & length: 1 970 clips × ∼10 s, ∼80K captions.
    • Task: clip captioning + retrieval.
    • Modalities: video only.
    • Probes: simplest possible single-event captioning.
    • Importance: deprecated (saturated); metric: same as MSR-VTT.
  • DiDeMo (see Section 14.4) — also a standard retrieval benchmark in addition to its grounding role; 10K videos, 41K text–video pairs.

14.7 Tracking, Detection and Segmentation Benchmarks

Closer to classic computer vision than to video VLMs, but worth listing because (i) representation models often pretrain on them and (ii) some VLM extensions (e.g. SAM-2, Mol-2Track) compete on them.

  • LaSOT (Fan et al. 2019) (2019) — single-object tracking; 1.4K sequences, mean 84 s; metric: AUC of success plot.
  • GOT-10k (Huang et al. 2019) (2019) — SOT with class-disjoint train/test (zero-shot tracking); 10K videos; metric: average overlap (AO).
  • TrackingNet (Müller et al. 2018) (2018) — SOT in the wild; 30K training + 511 test sequences; metric: AUC + precision.
  • MOT17 / MOT20 (Dendorfer et al. 2020) (2017 / 2020) — multi-object tracking; pedestrians; metric: HOTA (Luiten et al. 2021) + MOTA.
  • DanceTrack (Sun et al. 2022) (2022) — MOT for dancers (uniform appearance, complex motion); metric: HOTA + AssA — a particularly hard association benchmark.
  • DAVIS-2017 (Pont-Tuset et al. 2017) (2017) — semi-supervised VOS, ∼150 sequences × 4 s; metric: \(\mathcal{J} \& \mathcal{F}\).
  • YouTube-VOS (Xu et al. 2018) (2018) — semi-supervised VOS at scale; 4K videos; metric: \(\mathcal{J} \& \mathcal{F}\) split into seen and unseen classes.
  • MeViS (Ding et al. 2023) (2023) — motion-guided referring VOS; 2K videos, ∼28K motion-expression queries. Probes phrase-level grounding that requires temporal cues (e.g. “the bird that flies up”); a strong test for VLM-grounded segmenters. Metric: \(\mathcal{J} \& \mathcal{F}\).
  • V-HICO (Chao et al. 2020) (2020) — human-object interaction detection in video; 6K video segments, 244 verb categories. Probes spatio-temporal HOI specifically. Metric: mAP for interaction detection + temporal IoU.
  • DALY (Weinzaepfel et al. 2016) (2016) — Daily Action Localisation in Youtube; 510 untrimmed videos × ∼3 min, weakly supervised action localisation. Niche but instructive: weakly supervised setup mirrors what we have at web scale. Metric: video-mAP + frame-mAP.

14.8 Egocentric Benchmarks

Distinct enough from the third-person setting that it warrants its own section. The dominant data source is Ego4D, and most egocentric papers report on its native task suite plus EgoSchema for long-form reasoning (see Section 14.2).

  • Ego4D (Grauman et al. 2022) (2022)
    • Domain: egocentric video from 9 countries, daily activities (cooking, crafts, sport, driving, …).
    • Size & length: ∼3 670 h across 931 unique camera-wearers, clips up to 10 h.
    • Task: a 5-benchmark suite — Episodic Memory, Hands-and-Objects, Audio-Visual Diarisation, Social, Forecasting (each with its own splits and metrics).
    • Modalities: video + audio + IMU + gaze + 3D scans (varies by clip).
    • Probes: generic egocentric understanding at unprecedented scale; the data source for most modern egocentric pretraining.
    • Importance: critical; metric: task-dependent (e.g. Natural Language Queries — R@1 IoU; Object State Change Classification — accuracy).
  • EPIC-Kitchens-100 (Damen et al. 2022) (2020)
    • Domain: egocentric kitchen activity (cooking sessions, single-person).
    • Size & length: 100 h, 90K narrated action segments, 97 verbs × 300 nouns.
    • Task: action recognition (verb / noun / verb+noun), action detection, action anticipation, retrieval.
    • Modalities: video + audio + narration.
    • Probes: fine-grained activity recognition in a constrained domain; strong hand-object interaction signal.
    • Importance: critical for any egocentric encoder (VideoMAE, V-JEPA2, InternVideo2 all report). Metric: top-1 / top-5 accuracy per axis.
  • Charades-Ego (Sigurdsson et al. 2018) (2018)
    • Domain: the same activities as Charades but filmed simultaneously from third-person and egocentric.
    • Size & length: ∼8K paired video pairs × ∼30 s.
    • Task: action recognition; the canonical dataset for studying third-person ↔︎ first-person transfer.
    • Modalities: video + audio.
    • Probes: view-invariance of learned representations.
    • Importance: niche but important; metric: mAP (multi-label) and view-invariance gap.

14.9 Pretraining Corpora

Not test benchmarks — they have no canonical held-out split — but worth listing because almost every model in Section 12.3 cites at least one of them as training data. Quality and scale here dominate downstream benchmark scores.

  • HowTo100M (Miech et al. 2019) (2019) — 136M YouTube-clip / ASR-caption pairs from ∼1.2M instructional videos (∼23 years of video). Noisy by construction; the original “scale beats quality” pretraining corpus.
  • WebVid-10M (Bain et al. 2021) (2021) — 10M video-caption pairs scraped from a stock-footage site. Taken down in late 2024 after a copyright dispute; pre-2024 papers that cite it are increasingly unreproducible. The standard mid-scale captioned-video corpus 2021–2024.
  • VideoCC3M / VideoCC (Nagrani et al. 2022) (2022) — ∼10M auto-matched (CC3M-derived) caption / video clip pairs; cleaner alternative to HowTo100M.
  • InternVid / InternVid-10M-FLT (Y. Wang et al. 2023) (2024) — ∼7M videos × ∼234M re-captioned sentences (re-captioned with multimodal-LLM judges). The standard “modern” video-caption corpus, used by ViCLIP, InternVideo2, and many 2024–2026 VLMs.
  • Panda-70M (T.-S. Chen et al. 2024) (2024) — 70M video clips × ∼ 1 caption each; auto-captioned by an ensemble of VLMs and filtered. The largest open captioned-video corpus as of 2026.
  • HD-VG-130M / HD-VILA-100M (Xue et al. 2022) (2022) — 100M+ clips at HD resolution for video-language pretraining; useful for higher-resolution work.
  • VAST-27M (Chen et al. 2023) (2023) — 27M video clips with omni-modality captions (separate captions describing video, audio, subtitles) — designed for video-audio-language pretraining.
  • LLaVA-Video-178K (Y. Zhang et al. 2024) (2024) — 178K videos with synthetic dense captions + 1.3M QA pairs; the de facto SFT recipe for late-2024 / 2025 video VLMs (LLaVA-Video-7B, Apollo, VideoLLaMA3 all use it).
  • ShareGPT4Video (L. Chen et al. 2024) (2024) — 40K human + 4.8M GPT-4V detailed video captions; used widely as a quality signal on top of larger noisy corpora.

14.11 Choosing a Benchmark: Practical Recommendations

The benchmark choice is forced by two things — what task we want to prove and what budget we have. The recommendations below reflect a specific model trajectory: an autoregressive image-LM in the Molmo / Qwen2.5-VL line, first extended to video frames, later turned into a contrastive representation model via token-average + contrastive head.

Stage 1 — autoregressive image-LM extended to video

Five-benchmark suite, ordered by priority:

  1. MVBench (Li et al. 2024). Short-clip MCQ over 20 temporal-reasoning task types. The fastest, cheapest test that proves the model has any temporal understanding above a frame-bag baseline. Run this first.
  2. Video-MME (Fu et al. 2024) (full + short split). The de facto industry-standard video VLM benchmark; lets us position against Qwen2.5-VL / InternVL3 / LLaVA-OneVision on a single axis. Report both with-subtitles and no-subtitles.
  3. TempCompass (Liu et al. 2024) (or TVBench (Cores et al. 2024)). A paired-clip test that catches the “looks at one frame and guesses” failure mode. Pick TempCompass for clarity, TVBench if we want to share an axis with VideoChat2 and Qwen2.5-VL.
  4. Perception Test (Patraucean et al. 2023) (validation split, MCQ only). Diagnostic-style breakdown into Memory / Abstraction / Physics / Semantics × Descriptive / Explanatory / Predictive / Counterfactual; tells us where the model is weak.
  5. LongVideoBench (Wu et al. 2024) (validation, MCQ only). Only if we have decided to invest in long-context training; otherwise expect near-chance on the > 15 min split. Defers to Video-MME-long if budget is tight.

What we are explicitly not running first:

  • No K400 / SSv2 — these are encoder benchmarks. We will get a number from K400 zero-shot if we ever turn the model into a representation model in Stage 2; otherwise it tells us very little about the autoregressive head.
  • No MSRVTT-QA / MSVD-QA — generation-style, saturated, and replaced by MCQ benchmarks above.
  • No ActivityNet-QA — same, with one exception: cite it once for continuity with the older video-chat lineage.

Stage 2 — representation model (token-average + contrastive head)

Five-benchmark suite for the contrastive variant:

  1. Kinetics-400 zero-shot (Kay et al. 2017). The canonical zero-shot action-classification number. Compare to ViCLIP and InternVideo2.
  2. MSR-VTT 1K-A retrieval (Xu et al. 2016). Both directions (text → video and video → text), R@1/5/10. The default retrieval leaderboard.
  3. DiDeMo retrieval (Hendricks et al. 2017). Shorter clips, simpler IoU bins; pair with MSR-VTT.
  4. Charades-STA moment retrieval (Gao et al. 2017) (if the representation supports temporal grounding via similarity-over-time). R@1 IoU={0.5, 0.7}.
  5. SSv2 zero-shot or linear-probe (Goyal et al. 2017). The motion-heavy counterpoint to K400 — if a representation has not learned motion, this is where it shows. Skip if the model is purely frame-pooled (it will score at chance by design).

If we add a long-video retrieval/grounding axis later, the canonical pair is EgoSchema (long-form egocentric MCQ) and ActivityNet-Captions retrieval (medium-length narrated web). For corpus-scale moment retrieval, the canonical pair is TVR (short-form, multi-modal) and MAD (long-form movie).

14.12 2026 Trends in Video Benchmarks

A compressed read of the field as the chapter is being written:

  • Saturated, still reported anyway: K400 (encoders > 90 % top-1), UCF-101, HMDB-51, MSVD-QA. These remain on every results table for continuity, but moving the needle on them no longer signifies a real capability gain.
  • The 2024–2026 short-clip de facto trio: MVBench + Video-MME + Perception Test. Almost every video VLM paper from 2025 onwards reports all three; if a benchmark is missing from a paper it is usually because the authors didn’t like the result.
  • Long-video has consolidated: LongVideoBench + Video-MME-long + MLVU form the de facto long-video trio. LVBench and HourVideo are the dataset-papers’ newer hour-scale tests, used mainly by long-video specialists.
  • The “true temporal” wave: TVBench, TempCompass, and Vinoground exist specifically because most 2023–2024 video VLMs can answer MVBench-style questions from a single frame or from world knowledge. Expect any 2026 paper that claims temporal understanding to report at least one of these.
  • Egocentric has its own axis: EgoSchema (long-form) + EPIC-Kitchens-100 (fine-grained) + Ego4D NLQ are the canonical trio; if a paper claims egocentric understanding it has to hit all three.
  • The grounded-MCQ frontier: NeXT-GQA’s grounding gap — the fraction of correct MCQs that are also temporally well-grounded — is the most informative single number for “does the model actually know when the answer is?”. The field is collectively bad at this and that is where the next gains will come from.
  • VCMR at scale: TVR remains the standard, but MAD and MomentSeeker have moved the frontier to long-form / multi-modal queries. Combined with the rise of LLM-based rerankers (UniME-V2 (Gu et al. 2025)), 2025–2026 sees retrieval pipelines become two-stage (coarse first-stage embedding retrieval → LLM cross-encoder reranker) rather than single-stage.
  • Pretraining-corpus shake-up: the take-down of WebVid-10M in 2024 forced a switch to InternVid / Panda-70M / LLaVA-Video-178K. Quality + re-captioning has displaced raw scale as the dominant axis; expect 2026 papers to spend pages on data composition rather than architecture.
  • What is not reported and probably should be: open-ended captioning under SODA_c (only ActivityNet-Captions and YouCook2 do this routinely); spatio-temporal grounding for VLMs (no good benchmark in 2026); fairness/cultural-coverage of egocentric data outside North America and Europe (Ego-Exo4D partially addresses this, an emerging story).
